A real app needs more than code. IQX lets you deploy backing services directly
into your project, inside your project's own private network, with credentials
wired in automatically.

There are no forced integrations and no third-party accounts to create. The
services run for you, on our infrastructure, as part of your project.

## What you can deploy

<CardGroup cols={3}>
  <Card title="PostgreSQL" icon="database">

    what you get unless you say otherwise.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Redis" icon="bolt">
    Cache, sessions, queues, and anything that needs to be fast and temporary.
  </Card>
  <Card title="MinIO" icon="box">
    S3-compatible object storage for uploads, images, and files.
  </Card>

Other engines including MySQL, MariaDB, MongoDB, and vector stores are available
from the service catalogue in the Studio.

## How connection works

This is the part people expect to be painful. It is not.

When a service starts, IQX injects its credentials into your app as environment
variables. Your code reads `DATABASE_URL` and it is already correct. There is no
copying passwords, no `.env` editing, and no first-run migration debugging.

<Note>
Services are reachable from your app over the project's **internal** network
address. An external address exists too, for connecting a local SQL client while
you develop.
</Note>

## Autoscaling

Services scale by replica, following your traffic rather than a fixed size you
guessed at signup. Idle projects scale down, busy ones scale up.

## Managing services

Open the **Services** panel in the Studio to see everything running in the
project. Each service shows its status, its resource allocation, its connection
details, and its environment keys.

The same services appear as nodes on the **canvas**, where you can see how they
connect to your app visually and link them to other parts of the project.

<Warning>
Treat connection strings as secrets. The Services panel masks credentials behind
a show and hide toggle, and deployed apps receive them as environment variables
rather than in source code. Never commit them to a repository.
</Warning>
